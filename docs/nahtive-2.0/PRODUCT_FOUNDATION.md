# Nahtive 2.0 — Product Foundation

## Product statement

Nahtive is a structured Spanish-learning website that helps learners move from understanding Spanish to using it independently in everyday communication.

**Working tagline:** Learn Spanish. Actually use it.

## Who it serves

- Independent learners who want a clear path and consistent practice.
- Learners who want structured instruction, measurable progression, and detailed feedback.
- Learners across CEFR A1–C2 who want practical, natural Spanish.

## What makes it different

Nahtive combines:
- A deliberately sequenced Spanish curriculum.
- College-style clarity without unnecessary academic language.
- High-frequency vocabulary and natural Latin American usage.
- Reading and comprehension in context.
- Grammar taught through sentence construction.
- Translation, correction, fill-in-the-blank, and open-ended production.
- AI feedback that explains the reason behind corrections.
- Progress tracking based on repeated evidence and retention.

The platform should teach transferable language skills, not train learners to repeat a fixed set of phrases.

## Spanish standard

- Initial language: Spanish only.
- Initial variety: broadly useful contemporary Latin American Spanish.
- Regional terms are labeled when relevant.
- CEFR range: A1, A2, B1, B2, C1, C2.
- No A0 course or level.
- Difficulty is determined by actual content and task demands, not the label alone.
- A learner who struggles receives targeted support while the lesson's intended level remains intact.

## Lesson experience

A typical lesson includes:
1. A clear objective.
2. Useful vocabulary and expressions.
3. A contextual reading or dialogue.
4. Comprehension checks.
5. A concise grammar explanation.
6. Guided practice.
7. Independent sentence construction or real-world production.
8. A short assessment.
9. Feedback and a targeted next step.

Lesson structure can adapt to the objective, but should remain intentional and coherent.

## Feedback standard

For each learner response, the tutor should:
- Determine what the learner intended.
- Distinguish grammatical errors from spelling, accent, register, and regional variation.
- Accept natural alternatives where appropriate.
- Correct the specific issue rather than rewriting everything unnecessarily.
- Explain the rule in plain English at the learner's level.
- Provide one focused opportunity to apply the correction.

## Visual direction

- Light theme as the default.
- White and soft-neutral surfaces.
- Blue-violet as the primary brand accent.
- High contrast and readable typography.
- Minimal, deliberate motion.
- Responsive layouts for mobile and desktop.
- No green accent system, cartoon characters, childish illustrations, or unnecessary gamification.
- Dark mode may be added later; it is not a first-release dependency.

## Product success measures

Prioritize:
- Lesson completion and return-to-learning rate.
- Improvement in objective-specific assessment performance.
- Reduction in recurring error patterns.
- Retention on delayed review.
- Independent production quality.
- Learner ability to progress to the next objective without relying on hints.

Do not use streaks or time-on-site as substitutes for learning outcomes.

## Product guardrails

- Do not show English or French as available courses in the first release.
- Do not generate lessons without a curriculum objective and level constraints.
- Do not claim mastery from a single correct answer or one exam.
- Do not penalize a learner for a valid regional or grammatical alternative.
- Do not publish AI-generated lesson content before validation.
- Do not migrate or alter production learner data until a tested migration and rollback plan is approved.
