SITUATION · Looking back on a decision
“I would've brought it up sooner if I'd known.”
You are explaining that more information would have changed what you did.
✳ Why it works

“would've brought” describes the imagined result; “if I'd known” gives the unreal past condition. Together, they connect a different past situation with a different outcome.

The lesson method

Understand it. Build it. Use it for real.

Each lesson connects the explanation to the practice. You don't just see a phrase, answer a quiz, and move on without understanding what happened.

01

Meet it in context

Start with a realistic situation and language that fits the level—not a random list of disconnected phrases.

02

Understand the form

See what key verbs mean, how they're formed, and why that tense or construction fits.

03

Build your own sentence

Reconstruct a useful sentence and practice the structure instead of only recognizing the answer.

04

Check comprehension

Answer questions that test the actual lesson point, with feedback that explains the difference.

05

Use it naturally

Respond to a believable real-world prompt using what you just learned, in your own words.

Not just translation

Know why you say it that way.

Memorizing a translation won't always tell you how to make the next sentence. Nahtive's lesson structure makes the language itself understandable.
